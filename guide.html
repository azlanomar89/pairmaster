1. Getting started

PairMaster is a single file, PairMaster.html. Nothing is installed and no internet connection is needed: double-click the file and it opens in your web browser.

  • Best in Google Chrome or Microsoft Edge (Windows or Mac). These let PairMaster save straight into a folder you choose.
  • Safari and Firefox also work, but every saved file goes to your Downloads folder.
  • Use a laptop or desktop. Phones and tablets are not supported.

The screen has four tabs: Players, Rounds & scores, Standings and Print. Divisions appear as buttons under the tabs; click one to switch.

New to directing? Click Practice (top right). It opens a made-up event with two divisions where you can draw rounds, click Fill in random scores to play a round in a second, and try designated results, forfeits, Undo, printing and the prize list. Saving and opening files are turned off in practice, and your real tournament comes back untouched when you click Leave practice.

2. Saving your work

PairMaster saves everything automatically inside your browser as you type. That copy belongs to that browser on that computer only, and is lost if the browser's data is cleared.

Save after every round. Save writes two files: the .TOU file for AuPair and the ratings officer, and a .pairmaster event file that also keeps everything a .TOU can't hold (schools, classes, prize settings, total rounds, Gibsonization, forfeits and scoring rules). To carry on on another computer, open the .pairmaster file.
  • Save: the first time, Chrome/Edge ask you to pick a folder (for example Documents → PairMaster). After that, files go there. The 📁 Save folder: … button shows which folder that is; click it only to choose a different one (it doesn't open anything).
  • Automatic backup (Chrome/Edge, once you have saved into a folder): when a round finishes, PairMaster saves both files again by itself and keeps a numbered copy such as KLOPEN26-R05.TOU in a PairMaster backups folder inside it. If the browser asks for permission again (for example after a restart), the yellow reminder bar appears instead until you click Save once. In Safari and Firefox files go to Downloads, so the reminder bar is used there.
  • Open: opens a .pairmaster event file, or a .TOU file saved by PairMaster or by AuPair. The tournament on screen is replaced (Undo brings it back).
  • New: starts an empty tournament.
  • ↶ Undo (or Ctrl+Z / Cmd+Z when you are not typing in a box): takes back the last change, such as a wrong designated result, a redraw, a cleared round, a score or even opening a file. Click it again to go further back (up to 60 steps). Undo forgets its steps when you close or reload the page.

File names follow AuPair's rule: up to 8 letters or digits, then .TOU (for example KLOPEN26.TOU).

3. Players and divisions

On the Players tab, set the date, title and how ties are ranked (by spread, the usual choice, or by total score).

  • Enter players in rating order, highest first. The first-round draw and table order depend on it. Type a name and press Enter, or paste a whole list (one name per line).
  • Ratings (optional): tick it to add a Rating column. Leave a rating empty for anyone not yet rated: they show as "unrated" and nothing else changes. Sort by rating puts rated players first, highest first, and keeps unrated players in your order after them; it only happens when you click it. When pasting, a rating can go anywhere after the name (Ann Lee, 1650, Hillview School). Ratings are never written to the .TOU file; they are kept in the .pairmaster event file and allow a biggest upset prize.
  • Classes for prizes (off unless you tick it): a Class column appears (for example Newcomer, Under 12). The prize list then shows the best player in each class. When pasting a list with both schools and classes, write Name, School, Class.
  • Avoid pairing players from the same school or club (off unless you tick it, for each division): a School / club column appears. Type each player's school next to their name. When pasting a list, put it after a comma or a tab (Ann Lee, Hillview School), or copy two columns from a spreadsheet. Capitals and extra spaces don't matter. See section 4.
  • ↑ ↓ move a player; Sort A–Z sorts by name.
  • + Add division creates another division (A, B, OPEN, SCHOOLS…). Before the first draw you can move a player to another division, remove players, or delete a division.
  • Names are up to 20 characters (the .TOU limit).

Fixed tables

Add @12 to a name to keep that player at table 12 (useful for a wheelchair user or a player who needs to sit near the door). Add @R for a fixed table near the front. These markers are not printed. Remove them before sending the file for ratings (see section 10).

After the draw has started

Players can no longer be removed. If someone leaves, tick Missing: they get a designated loss each round and are left out of the draw until you untick it. A player added late gets designated losses for the rounds already played.

4. Drawing a round

On Rounds & scores, choose a method and click Draw round N.

MethodUse it for
Australian drawThe normal Swiss draw: players on similar scores meet, with no rematches. You can ask for a “fresh start” from a later game (rematches from before that game are then allowed).
Lagged AustralianFrom round 3: pairs using standings from two rounds back, so you can post the next draw before the current round ends.
King of the HillUsually the last round: 1st v 2nd, 3rd v 4th… Rematches allowed.
Round robinEveryone plays everyone. The whole schedule is drawn at once.
Halves / QuartersTop half v bottom half, or 1st v 2nd and 3rd v 4th within quarters.
ChanceRandom pairings.

Players from the same school or club

When Avoid pairing players from the same school or club is ticked on the Players tab, the Australian and Lagged draws avoid pairing players from the same one, and so do round 1 and the Quarters and Chance draws. If the quarters (or halves) of round 1 would put two schoolmates together, PairMaster swaps opponents with the nearest game that fixes it, so a player may meet someone from the next quarter instead; the top half still always plays the bottom half. Rematches are still avoided first: schoolmates only meet when the only alternative is a rematch, or when one school has more than half the players left to pair. Players with no school can be paired with anyone. Untick the box to stop (the schools you typed are kept, in case you tick it again). To keep schoolmates apart only early on, fill in Keep schoolmates apart in rounds 1 to 3 (for example) next to the tick box; from round 4 they may meet. King of the Hill, Halves and Round robin ignore schools. A game between schoolmates is marked same school on the scores screen.

Schools are not part of the .TOU file, so AuPair and the ratings officer never see them. When you open a .TOU, PairMaster keeps the schools of players with the same name in the division on screen; on another computer, type them in again.

Round 1 has its own options: quarters (1st v 3rd and 2nd v 4th, or 1st v 2nd and 3rd v 4th), halves, or chance, based on the order you entered players.

  • With an odd number of players, the lowest-placed player who hasn't had a bye gets it. A bye counts as a win by 1 (shown in brackets in the standings).
  • Starts are balanced: PairMaster tracks who went first. An asterisk * marks who starts; click ⇄ to swap.
  • Change opponent… changes one pairing by hand; the two players left over are paired together.
  • Clear this round removes a round (and any later ones) so you can draw again.

5. Entering scores

Jump to a game: type a table number or the start of either player's name in the box above the scores (press / to get there) and press Enter: the cursor goes to that game's first empty score. After the second score, it goes back to the box, ready for the next result slip.

Typo guard: a score under 100, or a game won by more than 350, makes PairMaster ask "Is 45 right?". Click Cancel to correct it.

Who is missing? Click the "3 scores to enter" button to list the games still to come, with their tables.

Type each player's score and press Enter to move to the next box. The result (“Grace Lim wins by 45”) appears as you go, and the counter shows how many scores are still to enter. The Find a player… box finds a game quickly.

A score under 10 counts as not entered yet. If a score is wrong, just type over it.

Below the scores you can record the division's high word (the word and player, for example QUIXOTIC Ann Lee, and its score). The high game is found automatically.

6. Byes, designated results, missing players

Use Change… (or Nongame…) to give a player a designated win, loss or draw instead of a game (for example a forfeit). As in AuPair:

  • a designated win counts as a win by 1, a loss as a loss by 0, a draw as half a win with no spread;
  • their opponent is left without an opponent and is marked “Needs an opponent”. Pair them with someone using Pair with…, or give them their own result with Change…. Nothing is decided for them automatically.

Forfeits (WESPA Rule 2.3.6)

If a player forfeits, for example for lateness, choose X forfeits in the game's Nongame… menu. Both players get their result at once: the opponent wins by 100 and the forfeiting player loses by 100. Neither is rated. The margin can be changed under Players → Scoring rules. In the .TOU file a forfeit is written as a designated win and loss, which every ratings officer accepts, so AuPair's own printout shows it as +1 and 0.

Scoring rules

Under Players → Scoring rules you can also set what a bye or designated win counts for (normally +1) and a spread cap: the most spread one game can add to the standings (used in Thailand). These affect PairMaster's standings and prizes only; the .TOU file keeps the real scores.

Designated results count for placings, not for ratings, and are shown in brackets in the standings: for example 3 (+1) means 3 wins from games plus 1 from a bye.

7. Gibsonization

When a player has clinched a prize place and can't be caught, Gibsonization stops them affecting the race for the remaining places. In the Gibsonization panel (shown for Australian, Lagged and King of the Hill draws):

  1. enter the total rounds in this division for the whole event (not the round you are drawing: for a 12-round event enter 12, even when drawing round 11) and the number of prizes;
  2. choose who is Gibsonized: only a player who has clinched 1st, or anyone whose prize place is decided.

PairMaster lists who has clinched (judged on wins). With an odd number of players a Gibsonized player takes the bye; otherwise they play the highest-ranked player out of the prizes. If more players are Gibsonized than there are players out of the prizes, the Gibsonized players play each other (1st v 2nd, as TSH does), so nobody still racing for a prize meets one of them. Untick anyone you don't want Gibsonized.

A player marked Missing is normally assumed to win no more games, so they can't stop someone clinching. If absent players sometimes come back at your events, tick Missing players may come back: they then count as able to win their remaining games.

8. Standings

Playoff final (optional)

After the last round, 1st and 2nd can play a best-of-3 or best-of-5 final: on the Standings tab, choose the length and click Start a playoff, then enter each game's scores (Enter moves to the next box). A win counts 1 and a tie ½; the first to 2 (or 3) wins. If the match is still level after every game, the playoff spread decides, then the player who led after the rounds. The standings and the .TOU file are not changed; the prize list and printed standings show the playoff result. If the standings change after the playoff starts (for example a score is corrected) so that the two finalists are no longer 1st and 2nd, the playoff is not counted and a warning asks you to remove it and start it again.

Standings rank by wins, then spread (or total score, if you chose that). Use the picker to see the standings after any earlier round. The 1st–2nd column shows how many games each player started and replied in; it is shown in bold when a player is two or more off an even split, so you can balance starts by hand with ⇄ if you wish.

Projector view ↗ opens the standings in large type in a second window for the hall screen. Drag it to the projector screen and click Full screen. It updates by itself as you enter scores, and shows each division (and each page of a long list) in turn every 15 seconds. If there is a playoff final, its score and then the winner of the title are shown under the title on every page.

9. Printing

On the Print tab choose what to print, for all divisions (one per page) or just this one, then click Print…. To make a PDF, choose “Save as PDF” in the print window.

  • Share images (WhatsApp, Telegram…): pictures of the latest standings, round results and next pairings for each division, made for phones (1080 × 1350, large type, at most 15 rows each; long lists are split into pages such as "1/2"). Options: first name + initial only and hide schools. Save images puts them in a "Share images" folder inside your save folder (or downloads them); Share… appears where your browser can share files directly; Copy as text gives the same results as text for a message. Nothing is sent anywhere until you send it yourself. If you share names of children, tell parents on the entry form that results will be shared.
  • Pairings for the current round, with table numbers, or for any earlier round (to reprint a lost sheet).
  • Result slips: one slip per table, two to a row, with both names (* = starts), score boxes, a spread box and signature lines.
  • Results for any round, optionally with the standings after it.
  • Standings after the last finished round, or after any earlier round.
  • Prize list: the top places (set how many; this is the same number as Gibsonization's prizes), the highest win, highest losing score, high game, best spread and high word, worked out after the last finished round. Players tied on wins and spread share a place, and all ties are listed. Byes, designated results and forfeits don't count for the game prizes. With classes ticked it also shows the best in each class, and you can add tuff luck (three closest losses), lucky stiff (three narrowest wins), highest average and best over rounds N to M (for a day prize). With ratings ticked it also shows the biggest upset (only games between two rated players count), and after a playoff final the winner is listed 1st.
  • Scorecards: one per player, round by round, with blank rows for rounds still to come.
  • Wall chart: every player's results in a grid (prints landscape).
  • Full schedule for a round robin.

10. AuPair and ratings

PairMaster reads and writes AuPair 6.16 .TOU files exactly as AuPair does, so you can move a tournament between the two at any point, and send the file to your ratings officer as before.

Before switching to AuPair mid-event, make sure no game says “Needs an opponent”: give that player a pairing or a result first. AuPair itself garbles that marker when it reopens a file: the player then seems to play a high number such as 245. If you open such a file, PairMaster says so, shows that player as “Needs an opponent”, and you can pair them or give them a result.

Before sending it for ratings, open Print → Tools → Clean markers to remove @12, @R and (…) notes from names.

If you open a damaged .TOU file, PairMaster lists the problems it found (for example “opponent number 99 does not exist”) before opening it, and marks those games “Needs an opponent” so you can fix them.

11. Problems and questions

My tournament disappeared

The browser's own copy was cleared, or you opened PairMaster in a different browser. Open your latest .TOU file.

It says the autosaved tournament could not be shown

The browser's copy was damaged. PairMaster keeps the damaged copy, starts fresh, and you carry on by opening your latest .TOU file.

Files go to Downloads instead of my folder

Your browser doesn't support choosing a folder. Use Chrome or Edge, or move the files from Downloads yourself.

Is my data sent anywhere?

No. PairMaster has no server and is blocked from making any internet connection. Your tournament stays on your computer. The only link out is Help: when you are online it opens this guide's web page in a new tab, and nothing from your tournament goes with it.
